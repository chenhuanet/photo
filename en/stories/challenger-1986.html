Visual dossier · 1986-01-28

1986: seventy-three seconds

28 January: a seal changed the shuttle programme · Kennedy Space Center / Atlantic Ocean

A teacher in space

STS-51-L had seven crew members: Francis Scobee, Michael Smith, Ronald McNair, Ellison Onizuka, Judith Resnik, Gregory Jarvis and Christa McAuliffe, a high-school teacher from New Hampshire. She was selected through the Teacher in Space programme and was to teach students across the country from orbit.

STS-51-L 乘组官方合影，1985 年 11 月 15 日。

Launch

At 11:38 a.m. on 28 January 1986, Challenger lifted off from pad 39B. The temperature in Florida was near freezing. Before launch, engineers at the solid-rocket-booster contractor had raised concerns about the O-rings in cold weather, but the launch went ahead as scheduled.

挑战者号由履带运输车运往发射台。
1986 年 1 月 28 日，挑战者号升空。
上升阶段的 70 毫米摄影机画面。

Breakup

About 73 seconds after liftoff, flame escaped from a joint on the right solid rocket booster, the external tank failed, and the shuttle broke apart under extreme aerodynamic loads. All seven crew members died. Ground cameras and television recorded the whole sequence. The Rogers Commission later found that the cold had robbed the O-rings of their resilience, the direct cause of the accident.

挑战者号在升空约 73 秒后解体。
休斯敦任务控制中心，发射当天的画面。

Afterward

The shuttle fleet was grounded for 32 months. Large amounts of debris were recovered for the investigation. In September 1988 Discovery flew STS-26 and the shuttle returned to flight. Challenger changed launch decision-making, safety oversight and engineering communication, and altered the public's imagination of human spaceflight.
