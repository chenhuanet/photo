January 20, 2018: Taliban

The Taliban is a Sunni Islamist political and militant organization which has governed Afghanistan since 2021.

Origins

After the September 11 attacks in 2001, the United States demanded the Taliban surrender al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, who was residing in the country. Upon refusing to do so, Afghanistan was invaded by a US-led coalition and the Taliban government was overthrown, most of the group fleeing to neighboring Pakistan. Following the invasion, the Taliban regrouped and mounted an insurgency against the US-backed Islamic Republic of Afghanistan and coalition forces. After signing a peace deal with the US, the Taliban conducted a nationwide offensive in 2021 culminating in the fall of Kabul, the collapse of the Islamic Republic, and the reinstatement of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan. The ideology of the Taliban is dominated by the Deobandi movement of Hanafi Islamic fundamentalism, along with elements of Pashtun nationalism. It is de facto the sole legal political force in the country, rejecting the principles of democracy and political parties. The group operates centrally under a supreme leader (Hibatullah Akhundzada since 2016) who wields unlimited power and issues fatwas serving both as law and religious instruction. The Taliban has been condemned for civilian massacres, human rights abuses and restrictions, particularly of women's rights, and persecuting ethnic and religious minorities. Unlike al-Qaeda and the Islamic State, it does not hold transnational aims—it remains discreetly allied with the former and is openly hostile to the latter.

The event

In English, the spelling Taliban has gained predominance over the spelling Taleban. In American English, the definite article is used; the group is referred to as "the Taliban" rather than "Taliban". In English-language media in Pakistan, the definite article is always omitted. Both Pakistani and Indian English-language media tend to name the group "Afghan Taliban", thus distinguishing it from the Pakistani Taliban. Additionally, in Pakistan, the word Talibans is often used when referring to more than one Taliban member. The group is also sometimes referred to as the Taliban Islamic Movement or Islamic Movement of Taliban, and often refers to itself by the name of the government it operates, the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan. In Afghanistan, the Taliban is frequently called the گرُوهِ طَالِبَانْ (Goroh-e Taleban), a Dari term which means 'Taliban group'. As per Dari/Persian grammar, there is no "the" prefix. Meanwhile, in Pashto, a determiner is normally used and as a result, the group is normally referred to as per Pashto grammar: دَ طَالِبَانْ (Da Taliban) or دَ طَالِبَانُو (Da Talibano).

Aftermath

Soviet intervention in Afghanistan (1978–1992) After the Soviet Union intervened and occupied Afghanistan in 1979, Islamic mujahideen fighters waged a war against Soviet forces. During the Soviet–Afghan War, nearly all of the Taliban's original leaders had fought for either the Hezb-i Islami Khalis or the Harakat-i Inqilab-e Islami factions of the Mujahideen.
