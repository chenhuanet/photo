1998: the first piece of the space station

20 November: Zarya lifts off · Baikonur / Low Earth Orbit

The first module

On 20 November 1998 the Zarya functional cargo block lifted off from Baikonur. It was the first module of the station, built by Russia with American funding, providing early power, propulsion, guidance and communications. Without it, later modules had nothing to dock to and no power.

第一个国际空间站舱段“曙光号”（Zarya）。
曙光号接近奋进号航天飞机。
云层之上的曙光号。

Unity

On 4 December 1998 Endeavour lifted off on STS-88 carrying the Unity node in its payload bay. The crew was Robert Cabana, Frederick Sturckow, Jerry Ross, James Newman, Nancy Currie and Sergei Krikalev.

“团结号”节点舱在奋进号货舱内。
机械臂把团结号举出货舱。
团结号与 STS-88 乘组。

Assembly in orbit

On 6 December the crew used the robotic arm to dock Unity with Zarya. Ross and Newman then made three spacewalks to connect power and data lines. For the first time the station had two modules, and “assembly” became a thing.

曙光号与团结号在货舱内对接完成。
轨道上的团结号与曙光号。
从奋进号拍摄的曙光号。

Afterward

In July 2000 the Zvezda service module joined; in November the first long-duration crew arrived and the station became continuously inhabited. Over the next two decades modules and experiments from many countries docked, and the ISS became the largest human-made structure in low Earth orbit.

2000 年，“星辰号”服务舱加入后的空间站。
2000 年，国际空间站拍摄的第一张地球照片。
