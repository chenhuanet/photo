Visual dossier · 1937-05-27

1937: the Golden Gate Bridge opens

27 May: the ferry era ends · San Francisco, USA

The strait

The Golden Gate separates the San Francisco peninsula from Marin County. Before the bridge, the two shores depended mainly on ferries. Work began in January 1933. Engineer Joseph Strauss led the project; Irving Morrow shaped the Art Deco towers.

1900 年前后，从电报山方向看金门海峡与马林县。那时还没有桥。
金门大桥早期施工，桥塔从堡垒角上方升起。

Construction

The towers stood on either side of the strait, with a main span of about 1,280 metres. Workers laboured in strong wind and thick fog as the cables were drawn from shore to shore. In February 1937 a section of scaffolding fell into the strait, killing ten workers; eleven died on the project in all. A safety net below the bridge saved nineteen lives.

Opening

On 27 May 1937 the bridge opened. Pedestrians crossed first, motor vehicles the next day. President Roosevelt pressed a key in Washington as the opening signal. The bridge was painted “International Orange” so it would be easier to see in San Francisco's fog.
