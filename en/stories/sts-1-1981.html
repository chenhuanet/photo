1981: Columbia's first flight

12 April: the shuttle era begins · Kennedy Space Center / Edwards AFB

A new spacecraft

The shuttle was the world's first reusable orbital vehicle. Columbia was the first to fly, its body covered with tens of thousands of thermal tiles. STS-1 was its first orbital test flight.

Liftoff

On 12 April 1981 Columbia lifted off from Kennedy Space Center with John Young and Robert Crippen aboard. It was the shuttle's first flight into orbit and a high-risk test.

1981 年 4 月 12 日，STS-1 升空。

Flight

Columbia flew for about 54 hours and circled Earth 37 times. It proved the shuttle's launch, on-orbit and return capabilities, and exposed problems with the thermal tiles and control systems.

约翰·杨和罗伯特·克里彭在 STS-1 着陆后走出哥伦比亚号。

Landing

On 14 April Columbia landed on the dry lakebed at Edwards Air Force Base. The shuttle era began; over the next three decades it deployed satellites, serviced Hubble and helped build the International Space Station.
